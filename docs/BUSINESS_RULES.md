# Methodology
## Data generation
Seeded NumPy generator; 120 daily observations, eight stores, four Indian cities and forty SKUs. Weekend and gradual time weights determine daily order counts. Hourly demand concentrates in evening periods. Product probabilities follow a skewed velocity distribution. Promotions increase basket line count. Price varies by category, tier, pack, store and seasonal factor.

Daily opening stock carries from the prior close. Explicit receipts increase stock. Simplified expiry removes aged stock. Requested units consume stock in time order. Unmet original-SKU demand may accept the next SKU within the same category; replacement stock is depleted separately. Unaccepted/unavailable units remain unfulfilled. An order cancels only if no units can be fulfilled. This model does not simulate customer-initiated cancellation or post-dispatch returns.

## Quality repairs
One deliberately missing payment method becomes Unknown. A malformed status is normalized. A missing customer reference maps to an explicitly added Unknown member. A malformed order timestamp is reconstructed from date/hour, losing minute precision. An invalid product category is recovered from the one-category supplier mapping specific to this simulation. A delivery-distance outlier uses the valid median; recorded costs are retained, so this imputed distance must not be treated as the precise original cost driver. Duplicate orders retain the first record. Additional unexpected integrity defects fail validation.

## Availability and loss
Opening availability = count of store/SKU/day observations with opening + receipts - wastage > 0 / eligible observations. This is not intraday availability. Stockout rate is its complement for nonempty data. Fill rate = fulfilled requested units / requested units; accepted replacements count as fulfilled. Lost units = requested - fulfilled. Estimated lost revenue uses requested selling price after the assigned discount. Lost gross margin subtracts original requested SKU unit cost. Recovery opportunity applies a 65% explicitly hypothetical recovery factor; it is not recognized revenue and excludes incremental recovery costs.

## Profitability
Gross sales = fulfilled units times charged requested-SKU price, including accepted replacements at that price. Discount applies only to fulfilled units. Net sales = gross sales - discount. COGS uses original and replacement product cost separately. Gross profit = net sales - COGS. Variable cost = delivery + packaging + fulfillment + promotion administration + payment. Discount is not deducted a second time as promotion cost. Contribution = gross profit - variable cost. Costs are allocated across order lines in proportion to requested units, including cancelled-line handling. Product-filtered contribution uses that fixed allocation. Wastage is reported separately at cost and is NOT deducted in merchandise contribution; neither fixed rent, tax nor payroll is modeled. This is contribution before inventory write-offs, not net company profit.

## Inventory
Last snapshot at selected date; demand mean/std from the trailing 28 calendar days, including zero-demand observations. Planning demand is the greater of recent seven-day mean and trailing mean. Safety stock = ceil(1.65 × daily demand standard deviation × sqrt(lead days)). Reorder point = ceil(planning demand × lead days + safety stock). Target = ceil(planning demand × (lead days + 7-day review) + safety stock). If stock <= reorder point, suggested reorder is max(0, target-stock). Confirm inbound stock before acting: open purchase orders are not modeled in recommendations.

Days of inventory = current units / average daily requested demand, undefined for zero demand. Period inventory turnover uses units sold / mean closing units over 28 days. It is not annualized accounting turnover. Dead stock: stock positive and no demand in that window. Critical: zero stock. Expiry risk: coverage exceeds nominal shelf life; this is a proxy, not batch-expiry prediction. Low: coverage below lead-time plus safety requirement. Overstock: stock above target. Otherwise healthy. Rules apply in that precedence order. Actions: confirm shortage/replenish, check batches/transfer, reduce buying, or monitor.

Velocity uses demand per SKU/store/day: below 0.1 very slow, 0.1–1 slow, 1–5 medium, above 5 fast. These transparent demonstration thresholds describe replenishment workload, not universal retail benchmarks.

## Forecasting
Daily requested units, not censored sales, are forecast. Final 14 days are held out. The preceding 14 days select among last-value naive, seven-day moving average, exponential smoothing with fixed alpha 0.3, and seven-day seasonal naive. Selection minimizes validation MAE. Each model is evaluated on the untouched test period using MAE, RMSE, WAPE and signed bias. WAPE is marked undefined when actual demand is all zero. Future forecasts refit the selected model using all history. Approximate uncertainty bands use the validation absolute-error 90th percentile; these are not guaranteed coverage intervals. Weekly forecasts aggregate daily predictions; aggregate and SKU models are not hierarchically reconciled.

## Risks and actions
Stock risk score = clip(100 × (1 - stock / (lead-time planning demand + safety)), 0,100). It is a deficit index, NOT a probability. Levels: <=25 low, <=50 medium, <=75 high, otherwise critical. Expected depletion date assumes steady planning demand and no new receipts. Revenue and margin at risk value the positive lead-time deficit at list price and list gross margin. These future exposures differ from historical lost-revenue estimates and may overlap with other actions. Never add all action impacts as an aggregate benefit.

## Promotions and statistics
Promotion AOV and profit differences compare exposed orders with all unexposed orders. Estimated incremental totals scale the observed mean difference by exposed orders. ROI divides the estimated contribution difference by discount expenditure; zero expenditure is displayed as zero with an explicit undefined-denominator convention. These are counterfactual proxies, not causal estimates. Welch testing is descriptive: repeated customers violate strict independence. The large-sample confidence interval is for observed AOV difference, not campaign causal lift.

## Scenarios
Demand growth scales requested units and baseline variable costs. Effective fill is min(selected fulfillment rate, availability cap). Price/AOV change scales realized unit price at fixed mix; discount replaces the effective discount rate. Unit cost stays fixed. Delivery-cost change affects only allocated delivery cost. Estimated inventory requirement = daily modeled demand × lead days. These are modeled estimates, not optimization or forecasts. At zero historical fulfillment, realized unit economics cannot be learned and the model returns zero unit economics; use a nonempty fulfilled slice for decisions.

## Live simulation
A separate SQLite database starts from historical closing stock. Each refresh transaction creates completed synthetic order lifecycles, inventory movements and explicit event rows. Simulation time advances to completion; this is accelerated lifecycle simulation, not concurrent dispatch. Every batch has a unique key; duplicate retries are ignored. Start/pause controls ingestion; speed controls orders per tick. Reset rebuilds only live data. A single-user local workflow is assumed. Live mode has no substitutions, expiry aging or production ingestion; canonical fulfillment and financial formulas are shared. Forecasting requires at least 42 live calendar days. Stock risk is recalculated from the live observation window and is unstable during warm-up.

## Validation contract
Primary keys unique; all foreign keys valid; nonnegative prices, costs, units and inventory; ordered = fulfilled + unfulfilled; fulfilled = original + substituted; daily inventory balance and continuity; delivery timestamp follows order; status follows positive fulfillment. Clean-data validation rejects missing snapshots.
